The Biggest Rewrite in
Two Decades.

A new core product safety framework and a consolidated enforcement toolkit are both moving right now — replacing rules written in 2005 for a world before online marketplaces existed.

JurisdictionUnited Kingdom (Great Britain + Northern Ireland provisions)
VerticalAll products — consumer and, newly, business products
StageMap — tracked in the Radar
Last reviewed15 Jul 2026
In thirty seconds
The plain version
  • The Product Regulation and Metrology Act 2025 received Royal Assent in July 2025, granting government the power to overhaul product safety law through secondary legislation — without needing a new Act of Parliament for each change.
  • On 31 March 2026, two linked consultations were published under that Act — one proposing a new core product safety framework, one proposing a consolidated market surveillance and enforcement toolkit — together described by government as the most significant overhaul of UK product safety in two decades.
  • Both consultations closed on 23 June 2026. As of this review, the government has committed to publishing a joint response within 12 weeks — expected around mid-September 2026 — after which secondary legislation follows.
  • The reform explicitly targets the gap the current 2005 rules never anticipated: online marketplaces currently have no direct statutory safety obligations in the UK, unlike the EU's GPSR, which already regulates them.
Where this actually stands right now

Not yet law — but the direction is set, and the timeline is short.

Jul 2025Royal Assent

The Product Regulation and Metrology Act 2025 became law, creating the delegated powers government is now using to reform product safety rules without separate primary legislation for each change.

31 Mar 2026Consultations open

Two parallel, cross-referenced consultations launched: one on the core safety framework itself, one on market surveillance and enforcement powers. A third, narrower consultation on furniture fire safety ran alongside them.

23 Jun 2026Consultations closed

Both closed at 23:59 BST. Responses came in from consumer bodies (Which? notably pushed for a fully independent OPSS with real enforcement teeth), trade associations, and businesses. This is where things stand today.

~Sep 2026Government response due

A single joint response covering both consultations, committed within 12 weeks of closing. This will confirm which proposals move forward largely as drafted, which get modified, and the expected timeline for secondary legislation.

BeyondSecondary legislation

Government will draft and lay secondary legislation under the 2025 Act, with implementation guidance and transitional arrangements for products already compliant under the current rules.

Verdict

Nothing here is enacted law yet — but the direction of travel is about as clear as a pre-legislative reform gets. Building compliance plans as though the current 2005 rules are permanent would be a mistake; building them entirely around proposals that could still shift in the government's response would be premature. The sensible position is watching the response closely and being ready to move once it lands.

What's actually being proposed

Two consultations, read together.

The core safety framework
  • Expanded scope: beyond consumer products, to cover "business products" too — industrial machinery, commercial equipment, professional tools supplied to businesses and public bodies.
  • New safety-assessment factors: cybersecurity risk, AI and machine-learning functionality — considerations the 2005 rules never contemplated.
  • Essential Safety Requirements (ESRs) for higher-risk product categories, potentially requiring third-party conformity assessment rather than self-certification alone.
  • Digital-by-default labelling, giving businesses more flexibility in how required product information is displayed — physically or digitally.
Market surveillance & enforcement
  • A single, consolidated enforcement toolkit — replacing fragmented powers currently spread across more than 150 separate pieces of product safety and metrology legislation.
  • Express statutory obligations on online marketplaces for the first time — closing a gap consumer group investigations have specifically flagged, including one finding that 85% of toys bought from third-party marketplace sellers were unsafe.
  • New civil monetary penalties, applied consistently across sectors, rather than today's patchwork of sector-specific enforcement mechanisms.
  • Consideration of whether OPSS should become a fully independent statutory regulator — a structural question still open in the responses submitted.
Why now — the gap the government is naming directly

The current rules were written for a world before marketplace selling existed at scale.

The government's own framing is blunt: the existing regime, rooted in the General Product Safety Regulations 2005, has been "stretched to its limit" by the growth of e-commerce, globalised supply chains, and emerging technologies. Unlike the EU's GPSR — which already imposes direct obligations on online marketplace providers — Great Britain currently has no equivalent statutory requirement. A consumer-group investigation specifically found it possible to list, and re-list, an unsafe product across multiple major online marketplaces even after removal, and a toy-industry investigation found the large majority of third-party marketplace toy listings tested were unsafe.

Lithium-ion battery safety — specifically e-bike and e-scooter fires — is called out directly as a live concern driving the reform, alongside the broader recognition that AI-enabled products don't fit cleanly into a framework written decades before such products existed.

Verdict

This reform is being built explicitly to mirror, in spirit, the direction the EU already took with its own GPSR in December 2024 — the UK government has said as much, noting a wish to stay "in step" with its largest trading partner even while writing UK-specific rules. Businesses already compliant with EU GPSR are likely closer to UK-ready than businesses that have only ever worked to the 2005 UK rules.

What it means for you, right now
Practical positioning during the gap between consultation and law
If you already sell into the EU under GPSR — your existing Responsible Person, risk-documentation, and traceability practices are likely to translate reasonably well once the UK framework lands, given the stated intent to broadly mirror the EU's approach.Government consultation framing, March 2026
If you sell only into the UK today — the current 2005 rules remain in force until secondary legislation replaces them; there's no need to pre-emptively comply with proposals that haven't been finalized, but building basic marketplace-facing safety documentation now is a reasonable hedge against the direction this is clearly heading.Product Regulation and Metrology Act 2025
If you operate an online marketplace — this is the single biggest near-term exposure in the entire reform; direct statutory obligations on marketplaces are one of the most consistently emphasized proposals across both consultations.Market surveillance and enforcement consultation, March 2026
