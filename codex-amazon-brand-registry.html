Own the Mark, Stop
Fighting Everyone Else's.

A registered trademark is the single prerequisite that unlocks listing control, brand tools, and priority-track enforcement — and a spring 2026 change just raised the cost of staying outside it.

JurisdictionAmazon marketplace (region-specific trademark required)
VerticalAny branded product sold on Amazon

In thirty seconds
The plain version
  • Brand Registry itself is free — the real cost is the trademark behind it. A US trademark generally runs $250–$350 per class in government fees plus $300–$1,000 in attorney fees, and takes 8–14 months to fully register.
  • Enrollment requires the trademark to be active, visible on your product or packaging, and consistent — exact spelling and styling matching across the trademark record, packaging, and your Amazon listings. A mismatch is the single most common cause of delay or rejection.
  • Once enrolled, you unlock Project Zero (self-service counterfeit removal) and Transparency (unit-level authentication) — both require Brand Registry first, and neither is available without it.
  • Starting spring 2026, Amazon requires Brand Registry to use manufacturer UPC barcodes with FBA — sellers without it must switch to Amazon's own FNSKUs for every unit, a meaningful new incentive that wasn't there before.
Eligibility

One prerequisite, applied strictly.

To enroll, you need an active Amazon Seller or Vendor account, a brand name or logo permanently shown on your product or packaging, and an eligible registered or pending trademark from a recognized government trademark office — the USPTO, EUIPO, and equivalents in most major markets. Amazon checks the trademark database directly: if the mark is inactive, expired, or under review without approval, the application is rejected outright.

The application must come from either the trademark's Rights Owner or someone formally authorized to act on their behalf. If an agency, employee, or business partner filed the trademark, they need to be formally listed as an authorized agent before completing the application — otherwise the verification code goes to a contact who isn't expecting it, which is one of the most common causes of enrollment delay.

Verdict

Consistency is not a nice-to-have — it's the actual test. The exact spelling and styling of your brand name must correlate across the trademark record, your packaging, and your Amazon listings before you apply, not after a rejection tells you it doesn't.

What it actually unlocks

A layered protection stack — each layer requires the one beneath it.

01
Trademark registration

The foundation. Without it, none of the following layers are available at all.

02
Brand Registry enrollment

Unlocks listing control (Catalog Lock prevents unauthorized edits), A+ Content, Brand Analytics, Sponsored Brands, and the Report a Violation infringement tool.

03
Report a Violation

Routes your infringement or counterfeit complaint on a priority track because you're the verified brand owner — but the quality of the complaint still matters enormously.

04
Project Zero

Self-service counterfeit removal — you take down infringing listings directly, without waiting in an investigation queue. Eligibility generally requires a sustained high accuracy rate (roughly 90–99%) on your prior violation reports over six months.

05
Transparency

Unit-level serialization — each product gets a unique code that Amazon scans before fulfillment and delivery, blocking counterfeit units from ever reaching a customer.

Verdict

Amazon's own reporting states its proactive controls now block the large majority of suspected infringing listings before brands even have to find and report them — but that automated protection scales with your accuracy history. A brand with a thin or inconsistent reporting record gets less of the automated benefit than one with a clean, well-documented enforcement history.

Building a complaint that actually works

Vague reports generate inconsistent outcomes. Specific ones don't.

A strong Report a Violation submission identifies the exact listing, the exact IP right being invoked, and the exact evidence supporting removal — not a general assertion that "this seller is infringing." The case file that actually moves consistently includes: screenshots, test-buy proof when feasible, side-by-side product comparisons, photographs of packaging differences, invoice or chain-of-custody trails, proof of trademark ownership, and a written explanation of why the specific offer is infringing, counterfeit, or otherwise unauthorized.

Typical Amazon response time runs 1–3 days once a complaint is filed, with full resolution often landing in 7–10 days when the evidence is clear and the accused seller doesn't counter-dispute.

The trap

Project Zero exists specifically for counterfeits — not ordinary trademark disputes over keyword use or design similarity. Misusing it for disputes it wasn't built for risks losing access to the tool entirely, or triggering account-level review of your own listings. Treat it as a scalpel, not a general-purpose enforcement button.

The 2026 change

FBA now costs more to operate outside Brand Registry.

Starting spring 2026, Amazon requires Brand Registry enrollment to use manufacturer UPC barcodes on FBA inventory. Sellers who haven't enrolled must switch to Amazon's own FNSKU barcodes for every unit instead — an operational cost and a labeling change that didn't exist before. Brand Registry was never technically required just to sell on Amazon, but this change meaningfully raises the practical cost of staying outside it if you're running FBA with your own manufacturer barcodes.

Separately, Amazon now allows a single global Brand Registry account to manage a multi-country trademark portfolio, rather than requiring a new enrollment for every marketplace — though the underlying trademark itself still needs to be valid in each specific region where you want enforcement coverage. A US trademark protects the US marketplace; selling into the UK, EU, or Japan still requires a trademark valid in each of those jurisdictions for Brand Registry protection to extend there.
