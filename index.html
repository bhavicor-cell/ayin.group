I Intent
I Finding I

Intent

The regulator does not ask whether you meant to comply. They ask what you built before anything went wrong. Dated policies, recorded decisions, systems that ran before the question was asked — not assembled after it. That is what intent looks like on paper.

If the record exists, the conversation starts from "this business tried." If it doesn't, it starts from "this business didn't care" — and everything after that is uphill.

Intent is read — never heard
II Reasonable Steps
II Finding II

Reasonable Steps

The test is not what you tried. It is what a careful business in your position would have done — an objective standard, answered only with evidence. What existed, when it existed, who was accountable for it.

Most businesses fail not because they did nothing. They fail because they cannot show what they did. An undocumented process and no process are the same thing in a hearing room.

The standard — not your story
III Willful Ignorance
III Finding III

Willful
Ignorance

You knew. Or you should have known. And you did not act. Malice is not required — inaction in the presence of knowledge is enough. Every day after a warning without a response builds the case against you.

This finding strips every protection the first two earn. A warning becomes a proceeding. An incident becomes an example made of your business. The only defence is a record that proves you were never looking away.
