The notice, decoded

What Amazon's language actually means.

Suspension notices are written in policy language, not plain language. Here's what four common lines are really asking for — click to expand.

What it means Amazon isn't accusing you of fraud — its systems flagged a signal (shared IP, device, warehouse, or payment method) that statistically resembles account linkage, correctly or not. The fix is proving separate ownership and operations, in writing, with documentation — not denying the connection exists when it does.
What it means This is the most common suspension type on the platform. Amazon wants a complete, traceable chain of custody — invoices from an authorised distributor, not just any receipt — and is comparing your documentation against what it already knows about that brand's supply chain.
What it means Section 3 is Amazon's most severe enforcement category — fraud, circumvention, and deceptive practices. These cases routinely need multiple documentation rounds and carry real risk of a permanent ban if mishandled. This is not a category to appeal casually.
What it means A mismatch between your registration details and your supporting documents — name, address, or banking — is enough to trigger this on its own. Amazon wants exact consistency across every document, not just documents that exist.
What it costs to get wrong

Most appeals fail for the same few reasons.

None of these are about luck. Amazon's review teams see your entire appeal history, so a weak first attempt makes every later one harder.

Generic root cause
"We always sell quality products and don't know what happened" reads as a non-answer. Amazon wants a specific, operational cause — the exact process gap that let it happen.
Promises, not proof
A Plan of Action describing what you will do is weaker than one describing what you've already done — with documentation attached.
Resubmitting unchanged
Sending the same appeal again, lightly reworded, after a rejection is visible to Amazon and reads as not having diagnosed the actual problem.
New account after suspension
Opening a second account is treated as a related-account violation in itself — it typically gets the new account suspended too, on top of the first.

Before you ask

The questions everyone asks first.

How long does reinstatement take?
It depends entirely on the violation and the quality of the appeal. A well-built first appeal for a documentation issue can come back in days; a Section 3 escalation can take multiple rounds. No honest party can promise a timeline before seeing the actual notice.
Can you guarantee my account will be reinstated?
No. Amazon makes the decision, not us. What we guarantee is the strongest, most accurate Plan of Action the facts support — built once, correctly.
What's the difference between this and writing it myself?
Amazon's reviewers are reading for a specific, operational root cause and verifiable documentation — not an apology. The work is in correctly diagnosing what the notice is pointing at, which is exactly where most self-written appeals go wrong the first time.
I already appealed and got rejected — will a new one make it worse?
A rejected appeal is visible to Amazon on every appeal that follows it, which is why a second weak attempt is worse than waiting and submitting one strong one. We review what was already submitted before drafting anything new.
Is this legal advice?
No. Ayin is an advisory and compliance practice, not a law firm, and a Plan of Action is a platform-policy document, not a legal filing. If your suspension has escalated into an actual legal dispute, you need a lawyer alongside this work — not instead of it.
