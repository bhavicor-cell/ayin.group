Who's Behind This

Krutarth E. Founder — Ayin & ORR A four-minute read

From an early age, I questioned almost everything. Literally anything.

Read the account
IThe question

Two things I could never stand:

01 information asymmetry
02 and people being exploited because of it.

I also disliked accepting something simply because someone — or something — said it was true.

Looking back, almost everything I've built has been, in one way or another, a response to those two things.

IIThe thread
01Chemistry

Curiosity about what sits beneath the surface led me to study chemistry. It wasn't just another subject — it felt like the foundation of almost everything.

02Environment · Canada

One area always pulled me in more than the others: the environment. I worked on environmental projects, presented them nationally and internationally, and eventually pursued Environmental Management and Assessment in Canada — wanting to understand what had actually led us to where we are, not just the consequences.

03Policy, then regulatory affairs

That degree pulled me toward law and public policy — researching them, writing policy papers, understanding not just what policies said but why they existed in the first place. It led naturally into Regulatory Affairs, looking at regulation through a policy and legal lens.

04The floor

At the same time I was learning how policies were designed, I was also working on the floor where those same policies had to function — perishables, baby products, electronics, inside large multinational companies, watching internal policy translate into everyday operation.

05The gap

Not because policymakers failed to understand reality — they usually do. They work within practical limits, competing priorities, politics, economics. But from the operational side, I could see how often policy became hard to execute because the infrastructure wasn't there, the process wasn't realistic, or the organisation simply wasn't built to support it.

Over nearly five years I met policy from almost every angle available to me — studied it, researched it, wrote about it, then watched people try to live with it.

IIIORR

That eventually led me to start ORR.

It began in Canada through personal connections and freelance work with local businesses, helping solve governance and operational problems. As things gained momentum, a serious illness forced me to stop, recover, and rethink where I wanted to build — it was close enough to a second chance that I don't take the work lightly now.

That recovery brought me back to India, where ORR became a proper firm rather than freelance work. Since then I've had the privilege of working with national and international clients, including several long-term partnerships.

IVThe pattern

Across different industries, I kept noticing something that refused to go away — and an order of building that felt backwards.

Governance failures were treated as operational failures.
Operational failures were treated as team failures.
Team failures were treated as individual failures.

The symptoms changed. The underlying problem usually didn't. At times it felt ridiculous — how could organisations this large operate like this? But I came to understand it: founders almost have to develop tunnel vision. Without it they never build anything. Growth demands focus, and focus leaves little room for governance until tomorrow suddenly becomes today.

Most companies build in this order

01Vision.
02Product.
03Team.
04Operations.
05Revenue.
06Compliance.

To me, that order always felt backwards.

Compliance shouldn't be the final layer added to an already-built business. It should quietly influence how the business is built from the start — not because regulators expect it, but because rebuilding foundations is always harder than building them properly the first time.

For the last several years, that's been the work I've enjoyed most — helping founders untangle these problems before they become something much larger.

Around the same time, I continued studying law through an LLB. It never felt like changing direction — it felt like continuing the same journey through another lens.

Chemistry→ Environment→ Policy→ Regulatory Affairs→ Operations→ Law

Eventually, everything
came together in Ayin.

An International Advisory and Compliance firm built around helping founders and businesses navigate international markets, regulatory complexity, and market access that often feels far more intimidating than it actually is.

If you've read this far, thank you.

Whether we ever work together or not, I appreciate that you've taken the time to understand how I think before deciding what I do.

Ayin wasn't built to sell compliance. It was built because I believe businesses deserve to understand the systems they operate within — not simply react to them when something goes wrong. That's the same belief behind the Codex and ORR's public systems: I'd rather reduce unnecessary obscurity than protect it, even for people who never become clients. And it's why, outside of work, I still pay attention to environmental policy — some things are bigger than any one person, which isn't a reason to stop caring about them.

We are not perfect, and we can't do everything ourselves. If you have a problem you think I might help with, I'd be glad to have a conversation.
